Zip Code Health Equity Report

Where you live shouldn't determine if you survive — but income, air quality, and insurance access vary a lot by neighborhood, and that affects real lung cancer outcomes. Enter any US zip code below to see live, publicly-sourced data for that area.

How to use this: if you're an individual, it shows you factors in your area (like air quality or uninsured rate) that may be worth mentioning to your doctor. If you're an advocate, it's evidence you can use to make the case for a mobile screening unit, an FQHC, or expanded outreach in a specific zip code.

"Time Off Work" Estimator

A screening appointment usually only takes a few hours. This estimates what missing that much work could cost you — so you can plan around it instead of avoiding the scan altogether.

Most people need 2–4 hours total: travel, check-in, the 10-minute scan itself, and travel back. Add more time if your clinic is far away.

Ways to reduce this: ask about early-morning, evening, or Saturday screening slots; check if you're eligible for job-protected leave under FMLA; or ask your employer's HR about paid preventive-care time. Some FQHCs also offer walk-in hours that don't require scheduling ahead.
